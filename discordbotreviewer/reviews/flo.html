All-in-one · FIELD NOTES 062

Flo wants server admins to manage Discord by describing what they need.

An AI assistant that previews changes before confirmation, backed by moderation, anti-scam tools, tickets, welcome messages and a dashboard.

DISCORD BOT REVIEWER EDITORIAL9 NOVEMBER 20265 MIN READ
FLO · AI SERVER MANAGEMENT

Flo's central idea is conversational server administration. Instead of navigating a long command list, an administrator can describe a change in plain English and let the bot prepare a plan.

Preview first, then confirm

Flo says it shows the proposed channels, categories, roles and permission changes before carrying them out. Nothing is meant to run until the administrator confirms, and its FAQ says destructive actions require a typed confirmation. That preview-first approach is useful for multi-step setup, although admins should still inspect every proposed permission change before approving it.

More than an AI builder

The public feature list includes moderation, automod for phishing and scam links, spam and mass mentions, anti-raid and anti-nuke protections, tickets with transcripts, welcome and goodbye messages, auto-roles, reaction roles, logging, custom commands, reminders, auto-responses, temporary voice rooms and a web dashboard.

Free and paid tiers

Flo's pricing page lists a free plan with core moderation, tickets, welcome messages, logging and limited weekly AI uses. Pro is listed at $5.99 per month and adds the ability to build a server from the AI plan, automatic compromised-account protection and higher limits. Ultra is listed at $19.99 per month with additional AI capacity. Confirm current pricing and regional billing terms on the official page before subscribing.

Privacy and trust considerations

Flo's privacy policy says message text is retained for seven days and images are not stored. It also explains that text sent to the AI assistant is processed by its AI providers, including Anthropic. That distinction matters: ordinary server messages and text deliberately sent to the assistant may have different processing paths. Read the full policy and decide whether your community is comfortable with those terms.

What to test before inviting

  • Confirmation flow: try a harmless multi-step request and inspect the preview before approving it.
  • Permissions: review the invite screen and place Flo's role carefully relative to roles it manages.
  • Automod: test false positives and configure the response to match your moderation policy.
  • Privacy: read the current privacy policy, especially AI-provider processing and retention.
  • Plan limits: check the latest quotas for AI uses, custom commands, voice hubs and sticky messages.

Our take

Flo's strongest differentiator is the confirmation-first AI workflow. It tries to make complicated server configuration approachable while keeping a human approval step between a request and the actual changes. The broad utility set makes it more than a novelty AI bot.

The main trade-offs are trust, permissions and data handling. An assistant that can create channels, roles and permissions needs careful review, and the privacy policy deserves a read before members use AI features. We have not independently tested its AI accuracy, scam detection or uptime.

Best suited to: administrators who want conversational server setup and a broad toolkit, and are comfortable reviewing AI-generated plans before approving changes.
